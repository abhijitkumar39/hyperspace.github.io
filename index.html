2024 – now · Backend and the match-day chatbot

Trantor

Visa Go · FIFA World Cup 2026

Trantor puts enterprise AI into production. On Visa Go, that was the backend and the chatbot for an invite-only app Visa's guests used at the World Cup. Visa sponsored the tournament. The app is how the guest list actually moved.

  • Visa's sponsored tickets covered about 30,000 people. Visa Go is where a guest accepted the place, built the trip, and asked what to do next.
  • Built the backend under that: tickets, schedules, and the path from "I have a ticket" to "where do I go."
  • The chatbot took the chat. Where the match is, when the tickets arrive, and the questions that show up the hour before kickoff.
  • Flights and hotels came back live through SerpAPI and Booking.com. A weather API and Google Places kept the answer tied to a ground, a forecast, and a place that was open.

Enterprise mobility for warehouses, stores, and delivery routes. Same muscle as the scrape years, pointed at the floor: an outside system, a pipeline that stays current, and an API something else can ask.

The scrape

National brands in. Our own model out.

These are the sites the spiders actually ran. Click one. Fuel, parking, homes, and jobs all took the same path, and the clean rows were what we trained on.

  1. Collect Scrapy on a schedule. Rotating proxies when a brand started blocking. One project per site, not a one-off script.
  2. Clean Raw pages landed in MongoDB. ETL typed the prices, places, hours, and listing text an API could serve.
  3. Train Those tables were the corpus for an in-house LLM. It learned our prices and places, then answered from that store.

The model was not trained on a generic crawl. It learned our tables: a pump price that moved through the day, a parking bay that was still open, a home listing that changed, a job post that vanished. Ask where gas is cheaper, or which builder still has inventory, and the answer comes from that store.

The public cousin of that work is a Telegram reminder bot that replies with a local Ollama model. Same idea, smaller: a model that answers from data we hold, not from the open web.
